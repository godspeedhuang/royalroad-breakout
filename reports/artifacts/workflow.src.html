RoyalRoad research workflow

This pipeline turns 1.61 million chapters into vectors for 45,245 books, to see whether hit tastes shift over time. Data and trend analysis are done; the main model, the AUC drift test and the direction explanation are not yet done.

Results so far

  • Supply is moving steadily.The cosine distance of the all-books centroid from 2016 grows from 0.017 to 0.035; noise is about 0.0003.
  • Preference drifts slowly.After noise correction, the "top 10% minus the rest" direction has similarity, 1 year apart, of 0.98, and 8 years apart, of 0.85. Using only 2019–2024 gives the same picture: within 5 years it falls from 0.95 to 0.88.
  • Drift is not just tags.Tags explain only 10% of the vector variance. After removing tags, similarity drops from 0.97 to 0.74.
  • 2016 tag data is unreliable.The median is 6 tags per book, versus 8 to 9 later, and tags like Progression only came into wide use in 2018 to 2021. This looks more like a change in platform or tagging habits than in taste.
  • The recent top 10% means something different.Books from 2023 and 2024 have short observation windows, so their top 10% is closer to books that took off early. This may also cause part of the drift and has not been ruled out yet.

Preference by tag

One row per tag, comparing 2017–2019 with 2022–2024. The x axis is lift: share within the top 10% divided by share among all books; above 1 means such books are more likely to become hits. The y axis is tag, sorted by 2022–2024 lift. The right column shows supply share in the two periods.

  • LitRPG is the most likely to hit, and still rising.Lift rose from 1.6 to 2.5, while supply fell from 31% to 22%.
  • Xianxia rose a lot.Lift rose from 1.3 to 2.2; supply stayed at 5% to 6%.
  • Romance fell clearly.Lift fell from 0.95 to 0.66; supply stayed at 19%.
  • Drama and Psychological returned to average.Lift rose from about 0.7 to about 1.
  • High Fantasy grew but stands out less.Supply rose from 15% to 38%, while lift fell.
  • Progression's decline should not be read literally.Early supply was only 1%, later 35%, more likely because the tag caught on.

These are correlations for individual tags, without controlling for overlap between tags (for example, LitRPG and GameLit often appear together). The chart also has no confidence intervals; tags with supply of only a few % have larger errors.

Design decisions made

DecisionChoiceReason
Research focusThe main model is the lead; the taste study plays a supporting role in act threeCourse grading looks at prediction, benchmarks and translating results into value
ComparisonLook at supply, hits and preference togetherHits may change only because the books on the market changed
Input textFirst three chaptersEvery book has them, and they were written before it took off; the whole book would peek at the outcome
CoverageAll 45,245 booksCost is acceptable, and no sampling to deal with
StorageparquetMostly batch reads for computation; vector database search is not needed
Judging trends1,536-dim numbers first, 2D plots as supportDimension reduction distorts distances; the PCA plane keeps only about 10% of the variance
Interactive toolEmbedding AtlasFiltering, search and raw text views are built in, leaving time for the main model
